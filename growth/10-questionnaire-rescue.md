# 10 · The Friday questionnaire rescue

A security questionnaire arrives on Friday and is due on Tuesday. Offer to turn
one around by hand, free, for ten startups. You get real problems, testimonials,
case studies and the best possible input for improving the guide. They get
their weekend back.

**Effort:** 10 rescues, about 2 hours each · **First signal:** week 2 · **Measure:** leads with `campaign = h10_rescue`

## Step by step

1. **Post the offer** (below) on LinkedIn and in founder communities that allow it.
2. **Take the first ten.** Say "full" publicly when you're done; scarcity is honest here.
3. **Ask for the questions only.** Many buyers forbid sharing the questionnaire,
   so ask for the question text without the buyer's name or branding.
4. **Deliver in 24 hours** using the gap analysis template.
5. **Ask for permission** to share an anonymised story, a week after delivery.
6. **Publish a case study** for every "yes". Use them in hacks 07 and 09.

This is done by you, by hand. Don't describe it as the agents doing the work.

## Offer post

> Got a security questionnaire due next week?
>
> I'll turn one around for free, for ten startups. Send me the questions
> (no buyer name needed), and within 24 hours you'll get back:
>
> → which ones you can already answer, with draft wording
> → which need an engineering change, and roughly how big
> → which need a certificate you don't have, and what to say meanwhile
>
> I do it by hand. Why free: I'm building a guide to what enterprise customers
> ask for, and real questionnaires make it better.
>
> Comment or DM "questionnaire". 10 slots.

## Intake reply

> Thanks, happy to help. So I can turn it around in 24 hours:
>
> 1. Paste or attach the questions, without the buyer's name or logo if your
>    agreement with them requires that.
> 2. Tell me the basics in a line each: hosting provider, whether you have SSO,
>    SOC 2 status, and whether the product has AI features.
> 3. Your deadline.
>
> I won't share anything you send, and I'll delete it when we're done if you
> prefer.

## Gap analysis template (what you send back)

```markdown
# Questionnaire review for [company]

Reviewed [N] questions on [date]. Deadline: [date].

## Ready to answer ([n])
| # | Question | Draft answer |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Do you encrypt data at rest? | "Yes. Customer data is encrypted at rest with AES-256 using keys managed in AWS KMS." |

## Needs a change before you can answer yes ([n])
| # | Question | What it takes | Say this for now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | Customer-exportable audit logs? | About 3–6 engineer-weeks ([link]) | "Audit events are recorded and available on request; self-service export is planned for [quarter]." |

## Needs a certificate or document you don't have ([n])
| # | Question | What it takes | Say this for now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | SOC 2 Type II report? | Six to nine months ([link]) | "Our observation period begins [date]; control documentation is available under NDA." |

## The three to fix first
1. [the gap that shows up most often in questionnaires]
2. [the gap that's cheapest to close]
3. [the gap most likely to block this specific deal]
```

## Asking for a case study (a week later)

> How did the questionnaire go? If it helped, would you be OK with me writing up
> a short, anonymous version, something like "a 20-person analytics startup
> answered 180 questions in a weekend"? I'll send it to you before publishing.

## Case study format

> **[Company type and size]** · [N] questions · due in [N] days
>
> **The situation:** [one sentence on the deal]
>
> **What we found:** [n] ready to answer, [n] needing a change, [n] needing a
> certificate.
>
> **What happened:** [outcome in their words, with their approval]

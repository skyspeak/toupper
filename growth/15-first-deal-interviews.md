# 15 · "How we closed our first enterprise deal" interviews

Founders love telling the story of their first big deal, and they'll share the
write-up with their whole network. Run a short interview series, one founder a
week. Each post brings in that founder's audience, mostly other founders, and
gives you real stories to check the guide against.

**Effort:** 1 interview a week, about 2 hours each · **First signal:** week 3 · **Measure:** leads with `campaign = h15_interviews`

## Step by step

1. **Build a list of 30 founders** who've publicly announced a first big
   customer: launch posts, "we just signed" LinkedIn posts, press releases,
   case studies on their site. Seed to Series A is the sweet spot.
2. **Send the ask below.** Expect about 1 in 4 to say yes.
3. **Do the interview by call or async** (some prefer to write). 25 minutes,
   using the questions below.
4. **Write it up in the format below** within 3 days. Send it to them for
   approval, and fix anything they flag without arguing.
5. **Publish on the day they can share it.** Tag them, and send them the
   ready-made post so sharing takes 10 seconds.
6. **Add what you learned** to the guide. If they say SCIM took 10 weeks, not
   6, change the driver or add one, and tell them you did.

## The ask

> **Subject:** your first enterprise deal
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> Congrats on [customer or announcement]. I'm writing a short weekly series on
> how startups closed their first enterprise deal: what the customer asked for,
> what surprised you, and what you'd do differently.
>
> It's 25 minutes on a call or a few written answers, whichever's easier. You
> approve the write-up before it goes anywhere, and you can keep the customer
> anonymous.
>
> Would you be up for it?
>
> [your name]
> (I build ToUpper, a free guide to what enterprise customers ask for.)

## Interview questions

Pick six. Always ask the last one.

1. What was the deal, in a sentence? (Size and name only if they want.)
2. When did you first realise this customer was different from your usual ones?
3. What did they ask for that you didn't have?
4. Which of those actually blocked the signature, and which were just asked?
5. What did you build, what did you buy, and what did you talk your way past?
6. How long did the security review take, start to finish?
7. What surprised you most about procurement or legal?
8. Did anything in the AI part of the review catch you off guard?
9. What did it cost you, in engineering time, that you didn't expect?
10. If a founder just got the same email from a big customer tomorrow, what
    would you tell them to do first?

## Write-up format (500–700 words)

```markdown
# How [company] closed its first enterprise deal

**[Company]** makes [one line]. [Team size] people, [stage].
**The deal:** [one line, anonymised if needed]. **Time from first call to signature:** [n] weeks.

## What they asked for
[The list, in their words. Link each item to its /what page with the h15 tag.]

## What actually blocked it
[1–2 paragraphs]

## Build, buy or talk past it
| Ask | What they did | How long |
|---|---|---|

## What surprised them
[1–2 paragraphs, with their best quote]

## Their advice
> "[answer to the last question]"
> — [name], [role]
```

## Post for the founder to share

> I talked to [your name] about how we closed our first enterprise deal:
> what they asked for, what blocked us, and what I'd do differently.
>
> Short version: [their one-line advice].
>
> [link]

## What to measure

- Leads tagged `h15_interviews`, and which interview they came from
  (`utm_content=<company>` on each link).
- Estimate corrections you made because of an interview. Mention them in the
  write-up: "we changed our SCIM estimate after this conversation" is a good
  line, and it's true.

# 05 · Estimate wars

Post a specific number people will disagree with, then invite them to prove
theirs using the estimate, which changes with their situation. Disagreement
drives comments; every "mine was different" is someone opening the link to set
up their own case.

**Effort:** 6 posts · **First signal:** week 1 · **Measure:** leads with `campaign = h05_estimates`

## Step by step

1. **Post one a week** on LinkedIn, and on X if you use it.
2. **Put the link in the first comment** (`h05_estimates` in [`links.csv`](links.csv)).
3. **Answer disagreement with a question:** "What made yours faster? Which of
   the factors didn't apply to you?" Never argue with a person, only a number.
4. **When someone shares their own configured link, repost it** with thanks.
   That's the point of the whole exercise.

## Posts

### SOC 2

> Unpopular: "SOC 2 in six weeks" is almost always a Type I, or someone's
> selling something.
>
> The engineering is weeks. The Type II observation window makes it six to
> nine months end to end.
>
> If you did Type II faster, I'd honestly like to know how. Set up your own
> estimate (first comment) and share what you ticked.

### SSO

> The SAML part of SSO is small. SSO for a real customer is six to eleven
> engineer-weeks.
>
> The difference is everything around the protocol: linking existing password
> accounts, letting customers enforce SSO, a break-glass login that security
> will approve.
>
> Tell me I'm wrong. The estimate's in the first comment; change the factors to
> match yours.

### "Just put it in their VPC"

> "Can we deploy it in their VPC?" is a pricing question disguised as an
> engineering one.
>
> If the customer runs it, not you, making the product installable and
> supportable blind is roughly 12 to 24 engineer-weeks, and it never stops
> costing you.
>
> Did you charge enough for yours?

### Usage-based billing

> Buying a billing platform doesn't make usage billing easy. It makes invoicing
> easy.
>
> Accurate usage events, commits and credits, and migrating seat customers is
> still 6 to 12 weeks of your own work.
>
> Anyone who moved to usage pricing in under a month: what did you skip?

### Evals

> If your AI team can't tell you whether the last prompt change made things
> better or worse, you don't have an AI quality process. You have vibes.
>
> A starter eval set from real cases is 2 to 4 weeks with a tool, 4 to 8
> without. Worth it before an enterprise AI review, not after.
>
> What does your team use instead?

### Custom roles

> "Can we add custom roles?" sounds like a settings page. If your permission
> checks are spread across the codebase, it's a quarter.
>
> Four to eight weeks for the model, plus four to ten to find and replace the
> checks you already have.
>
> How long did yours take?

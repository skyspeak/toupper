# 17 · The podcast and newsletter guest circuit

B2B SaaS podcasts and founder newsletters need guests with a specific, useful
topic every week. "What your first enterprise customer will ask for, and what
it really costs" is that topic, and ten pun-named specialists make it
memorable. Each appearance reaches thousands of the right people and stays
findable for years.

**Effort:** 20 pitches, 1 recording a fortnight · **First signal:** week 5 (shows publish slowly) · **Measure:** leads with `campaign = h17_guest`

## Step by step

1. **Make a list of 25 shows and newsletters** aimed at SaaS founders, early
   sales hires, product managers or engineering leaders. Find them by searching
   podcast apps for "B2B SaaS", "founder", "enterprise sales", "product led",
   and by checking who founders you follow have been guests on.
2. **Listen to or read one recent episode of each.** Your pitch must mention
   it specifically, or it reads as spam.
3. **Send the pitch below** to the host's published contact or booking form.
   One follow-up after 10 days.
4. **Send the one-sheet** to anyone who says yes.
5. **Prepare with the talking points.** Bring one number and one story to each.
6. **Give the host a tagged link** (`utm_source=<show name>`) for the show notes,
   and a short line to read out.
7. **Share the episode** on the day it goes out, and clip one 45-second answer
   for LinkedIn.

## One-sheet

```markdown
# [Your name]

[One-line credential.] Builds ToUpper, a free guide to what enterprise
customers ask startups for.

## Topics
1. **The ten weeks after the yes.** SSO in week 1, a 300-line questionnaire in
   week 2, a DPA in week 4, the AI review in week 5, procurement in week 6.
2. **Block or defer?** Which enterprise asks stop a signature and which can go
   in a 90-day roadmap letter.
3. **The enterprise tax.** A typical first deal asks for roughly 30–57
   engineer-weeks of work if you build it all, or about 12–26 if you buy where
   you can. Where the time goes.
4. **The AI review is the new security review.** Training on customer data,
   evals, prompt injection, and what buyers now send.

## Links
Guide: [tagged link] · LinkedIn: [link]
```

## Talking points to have ready

- **The number.** Eight common asks (SSO, SCIM, custom roles, audit logs,
  SOC 2, a security questionnaire, an SLA, enterprise invoicing): 30–57
  engineer-weeks to build, 12–26 buying where there's a platform. SOC 2 still
  takes six to nine months on the calendar either way.
- **The surprise.** SCIM is often harder than SSO, because removed users'
  data has retention and transfer rules nobody has decided.
- **The advice.** Before anyone writes code, get the exact wording of the ask
  and find out whether it blocks signing or rollout.
- **The honest part.** The agents are characters. The names are jokes; the
  estimates are hand-written ranges, and the invitation is to tell you
  when yours was different.

## Line for the host to read

> [Your name] builds ToUpper, a free guide to what enterprise customers ask for

## What to measure

- Leads by `source = <show name>`. Podcasts convert slowly, so look at 60 days, not 7.
- Which topic hosts pick. If three pick "the AI review", that's your next post
  series.

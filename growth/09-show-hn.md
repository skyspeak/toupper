# 09 · An honest Show HN

Hacker News punishes marketing and rewards honesty. ToUpper has an unusually
honest hook: the agents are fictional puns, and the page says so, but the
estimates and questions are real work. Lead with that.

**Effort:** 1 day · **First signal:** launch day · **Measure:** leads with `campaign = h09_launch`

## Step by step

1. **Launch after hack 10** has given you two or three real stories to mention.
2. **Post on a weekday morning,** US Pacific time.
3. **Stay on it for four hours.** Answer every comment, especially the critical ones.
4. **Never ask anyone to upvote.** HN detects and penalises it.
5. **The next day, write down every missing topic** people raised, and add the
   most requested one to the guide within a week. Then reply in the thread saying so.

## Title options

- `Show HN: What your first enterprise customer will ask for, and what it takes`
- `Show HN: A guide to SCIM, SOC 2, RAG evals and 25 other enterprise asks`
- `Show HN: The agents are fake, the enterprise estimates aren't`

The third gets clicks and some eye-rolls. The first is the safe choice.

## Post

> I kept watching startups sign their first enterprise customer and then lose
> months to the follow-up asks: SSO, SCIM, SOC 2, audit logs, a DPA, and now AI
> reviews about training data and prompt injection.
>
> So I wrote a guide to 28 of them. For each: what it is in plain language, a
> rough estimate in engineer-weeks for building versus buying (with factors you
> can tick to match your situation), and the questions each part of the org
> needs to answer before starting. Every answer has its own URL, so you can
> send your CTO exactly the view you're looking at.
>
> Two honest caveats. The estimates are my judgment of ranges for a Series A–C
> team, not measured data, and I'd like to be corrected. And the "agents" who
> answer are characters with pun names (Sam L. Assertion does SAML); there's no
> model behind them, just hand-written content.
>
> [link]

## Replies to have ready

- **"The estimates are made up."** They're judgment calls, and the page says so.
  Which one is off for you? Tell me the factor and I'll update it.
- **"Why pretend they're AI agents?"** They're characters, and the page says that
  in the first line. The names made it memorable; the content is written by hand.
- **"This is lead gen."** There's an optional "get help" form. Everything else
  is readable without giving an email.
- **"Just ask ChatGPT."** You'll get a different number each time. The point of
  this is a consistent estimate and a checklist you can forward.
